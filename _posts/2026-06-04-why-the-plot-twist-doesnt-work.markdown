**WARNING**: This blog post contains **SPOILERS** for the game, *Detroit: Become Human* (DBH). Please proceed with caution if you haven't played the game yet! (Or don't, it's entirely up to you.)

---------------------------------

I've seen the topic be brought up time and time again surrounding the "twist" that Alice is an android and not a human, especially on the Detroit: Become Human subreddit. And even though I see a lot of people in favour of the twist, I personally believe that the twist *does not work.*

The main argument that I have heard from the people that *do* support the twist is that it's supposed to be a test for a player; to see whether the player still views Alice as a person regardless of her being an android, or whether it changes the player’s perspective on her and causes them to abandon her.

And to be fair, I *absolutely* understand and appreciate that that’s what the game was *trying* to do.

However, there is a reason I am putting emphasis on the word, *trying*.

The way I see it, DBH is still a video game at the end of the day. Regardless of whether its gameplay fits that of a typical video game, or whether it plays more as an interactive movie than a video game, it’s still a *video game*; a video game where you are playing in the perspective of a specific, defined character (or in DBH's case, three different characters). When developing video games where you are playing as a defined character, it is very important to ensure that the player’s motives, decisions, feelings, etc. align with the character’s, and vice versa; because after all, the character is ultimately an extension of the player.

Now, I’m not saying that every single character in video games needs to be a self-insert per se – it *never* needs to go to that extreme – but at the bare minimum, the player and the character should emotionally and mentally be in the same boat so to speak, and if there is any mismatch in motives, beliefs, biases, etc. between the player and the character, it is going to break immersion most of the time, and as a result cause the game to feel "clunky."

And I found that this twist was an example of exactly that; if this twist was supposed to be a test for the *player*, it should also be a test for the *character*.

Now people can argue that it is also a test for the character, since she always ignored the fact that Alice was an android and would refuse to accept her as such. However, there are a few points I want to bring up:

**One:** There is really no obvious proof that Kara herself was ignoring the fact that Alice was an android prior to Crossroads (I will elaborate shortly on this), and, more importantly;

**Two:** There isn’t any real (believable) motive to *Kara* wanting to view Alice as a human like there is for the *player*, or any in-game explanation as to why she wants to (I'll elaborate on this as well), especially since she can say the below to Markus (when talking to him about Alice) right before the twist:

To elaborate more on my first point, there are many “hints” towards the twist that people have insisted make it "obvious," even though they aren’t necessarily obvious for everyone on a first playthrough; this ultimately results in this player/character “mismatch” that I was discussing earlier (and as I briefly mentioned too, I'm not listing these items necessarily as a way to support the twist or suggest that it was well-written, but more so rather to highlight that it wasn’t really obvious to everyone during a first playthrough (or, at least, wasn't obvious to *me*) that she was denying Alice being an android).

Some of these have included the following:

- The magazine in A New Home. I've seen a lot of people say that this was supposed to be the “most obvious” hint and an absolute “dead giveaway” of the twist, even though the magazine was blurred/obscured until the legible, non-blurred version was shown during Crossroads *on purpose*. In my opinion, it doesn't make sense to state that this hint was as obvious as it was, as it would be no different than telling a heavily nearsighted person how "obvious" it is that there is a bird in a tree 50 feet away, or telling a colourblind person how "obvious" it is that an object is red.
       
- Many people also argue that Alice never eating, or always refusing to eat, further “confirmed” that she was an android. Even though her being an android *was* the reason why she didn't eat, it's hard for me to believe that the player should automatically be assuming this is the case on a first playthrough; just because we didn’t specifically see her eat *on screen* doesn’t necessarily mean she wasn’t eating *at all*. Theoretically, by that logic, characters such as Kamski, Leo, Ben, Chris, Perkins, etc. who also were never shown eating on screen could also be androids, even though it’s very well established that they aren’t.

*(Now I have heard some headcanons/theories about Kamski uploading his consciousness into an android body and no longer being human, but that’s an entirely different story altogether that isn’t mentioned in canon and that I won’t get into here.)*

I mean this in the nicest way possible when I say that assuming a character is just not eating *at all* just because we don’t see them eat on screen is a fairly surface level conclusion; not to mention that there are other plausible reasons why she may have been refusing to eat, assuming she was human (ex. Maybe she refused to eat Rose’s spaghetti in Midnight Train because the events of Stormy Night made spaghetti triggering for her; maybe she refused the chocolate bar you steal in Fugitives because she didn’t want to eat something that was stolen (which it is shown during that chapter that she is very heavily against Kara stealing from the convenience store, so this would also make sense)). Kara’s one comment in The Pirates’ Cove chapter about how there are “cookies left” in Luther’s bag could also further imply that Alice may have eaten some of the cookies already while they were in the car on the way to the fun fair/Rose’s house:

{% include embed-audio.html src="/assets/audio/X0701K_SHOP_COOKIES_PC_X07KKARA_LIKESOME_ENG.wav" %}
**TRANSCRIPTION: "There are some cookies left. Would you like some?"**

There are a lot of reasons why she could have been refusing to eat on screen, and a lot of opportunities off screen where she could have potentially eaten. Especially considering that Kara was being “delusional” because of these “missed queues” according to the fanbase, it is also hard for the player to feel like they’re being “delusional” alongside her, or see Kara clearly being delusional, when to the player, there are *actual legitimate reasons/conclusions* as to why Alice behaves the way she does. This “mismatch” ultimately comes from Kara truly being “delusional” and *actively ignoring* that Alice is an android, and the player *actually assuming* Alice as human based on the evidence given.

It is also for this reason why I believe that Quantic Dream could have done a much better job writing Kara as delusional - this is also especially considering how clear it is that Connor was written to be delusional to the player, and therefore the potential Kara had to be written in a similar way.

The player also does have real, tangible evidence to initially assume Alice was, and to treat her as, human, early on in the game (her having a last name unlike most other androids in canon is one example of this), and also not to mention how much more dynamic it could have made both Kara and Alice’s relationship and narrative. I've heard from (and agree with) some players in the fanbase that have stated that they were so invested in their story initially *because* of this type of relationship, and how an android parent could raise a human child, how a human child could be raised by an android parent, how a human child would age quicker than their android parent, etc. Now, I do agree that Markus and Carl, as well as Connor and Hank for these types of dynamic android/human relationships; you could even theoretically argue that Kara and Rose’s relationship could be included into the mix. However, my argument to *that* is that if these relationships are already establishing that humans and androids can coexist, and are therefore sufficient for the player to recognise androids as people, then the extra “plot twist” with Alice, which is used to try and reinforce this fact, is therefore never needed, especially when it ultimately (tarnishes is too strong of a word) hinders Kara’s story as a result.

If I remember correctly, this was the only “hint” that I really caught on my first playthrough; and I put the word “hint” in quotes because when I originally heard it, I didn’t interpret it in the literal sense of, “Oh, she’s referring to herself as one of them, that must mean she’s an android!” – which would ultimately hint towards the twist – but rather in the metaphorical sense of, “Oh she’s referring to herself as one of them, because she’s been able to see the entire ‘Android vs. Human’ stance from an android’s perspective from essentially having android parents for a week, and doesn’t see any difference between herself and them as they are all just people at the end of the day.” And honestly to me, viewing this quote from this metaphorical point of view, while still under the assumption that Alice is human, actually would have made this one of the most significant quotes in the game, *especially* with it coming from a child, and in my opinion actually would have emphasized and honed in more on the message that the twist was actually trying to convey than the twist itself had.

One of the main pieces of evidence that I have heard suggesting why Kara actually wanted to view Alice as a human was because (and hopefully I get this right) if she did, then she wasn’t fully deviating, since she was programmed to be a mother to human children and not android children, and then the twist in Crossroads was her fully accepting Alice as an android, and thus fully deviating from her programming. What confuses me about this argument, is that it implies that Kara doesn't *want* to fully deviate in Stormy Night, or hesitates deviating, even though she has no implied motive for not wanting to. At least with Connor, he literally fears even so much as *expressing emotion* (and wrongly convincing himself that he *can’t*) because of the potential that Cyberlife can completely shut him and his model line down if he deviates, so it makes a lot of sense why *he* would hesitate deviating. As well, if anything, I would think she’d be *more* inclined to help Alice escape her situation if she was literally the same species as her, more so than if she was a different species that was especially known to *harm* hers.

Anyway, that is my rant about the Alice twist, and why I personally believe that it does not work.
